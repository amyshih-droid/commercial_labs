FDA registration coverage analysis: what is and is not covered for pharma, therapeutics, CMO, CDMO, and CRO — plus recommended data sources for each gap

What FDA drug establishment registration actually covers
Pharma manufacturers (finished drug products) ✓ covered
Any facility that manufactures, repacks, or relabels finished drug products must register. This includes both large pharma (Pfizer, Merck) and small specialty manufacturers.
Source:FDA Drug Establishment Registration (fda.gov)
CMOs and CDMOs ✓ covered — but mixed with internal
Contract manufacturers must register with FDA just like internal facilities. They appear in the same database — you cannot tell from the registration alone whether a facility is a CMO/CDMO or an internal-only plant. That classification requires an extra step.
Source:FDA Drug Establishment Registration + OpenAI to classify
Biotech / biologics manufacturers partial — separate database
Biologics (vaccines, cell therapy, gene therapy, monoclonal antibodies) have a separate FDA registration: the Biologics Establishment Registration. Many biotech CDMOs appear here, not in the drug establishment file.
Source:FDA Biologics Establishment Registration (separate download)
Clinical Research Organizations (CROs) ✗ not covered
CROs run clinical trials — they don't manufacture drugs. FDA regulates them under clinical trial rules (21 CFR Part 312), but there is no public registry or downloadable list of CROs. This is the biggest gap.
FDA inspects CROs but publishes no searchable directory. CROs are effectively invisible to public data sources.
Best source:OpenAI web search only — no free public database exists
Pure R&D / therapeutics companies (no manufacturing) ✗ not covered
A biotech startup developing a new drug therapy but not manufacturing it yet has no obligation to register with FDA. Many early-stage therapeutics companies are completely absent from FDA data.
FDA only requires registration when a facility physically manufactures or processes drug products.
Best source:SEC EDGAR (public companies), Crunchbase, OpenAI web search
What "internal only" means
Both types register with FDA — only one is a BD target
Internal facility — not a BD target
Pfizer's plant in Kalamazoo, MI
Makes drugs only for Pfizer
Does not accept external clients
Has no BD team or sales contact
Must still register with FDA
CMO/CDMO — your BD target
Lonza, Catalent, Thermo Fisher
Makes drugs FOR other companies
Actively seeks external clients
Has BD team, published contacts
Also registers with FDA
Both look identical in the FDA file. The only way to tell them apart is OpenAI web search: "Does [company] accept external manufacturing contracts?" — internal-only facilities have no marketing for external clients; CDMOs have dedicated BD pages.
Recommended source strategy per company type
What you need vs. what each source gives you
FDA Drug Establishment Registration → pharma manufacturers + CMOs/CDMOs (mixed, needs classification)
FDA Biologics Establishment Registration → biotech/cell/gene therapy CDMOs
OpenAI web search → classify CMO/CDMO vs internal, find CROs, find R&D-only companies
No free public source → CROs and early-stage therapeutics require AI-assisted discovery
Honest answer to your question: FDA data alone is NOT enough to cover all pharma/therapeutics/CMO/CDMOs/CROs. It gives you a solid foundation for manufacturers and CMO/CDMOs, but misses CROs entirely and misses pure R&D companies. Phase 2 needs two FDA downloads (drugs + biologics) plus OpenAI for CROs and classification. This is why Phase 2 is genuinely harder than Phase 1.
